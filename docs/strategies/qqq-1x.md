## Backtest Results

Rolling 26-year methodology per [Methodology](methodology.md).

### Table 7: QQQ (1x) — Strategy Comparison
*^NDX base at 1x, scaled to QQQ's 0.20% expense ratio (same convention as [Table 1](core-trend-signal.md)'s 1x tier — see caveats below), SMA 200 (ATR x2.5). Date range: 1986-04-29 to 2000-07-28 (172 rolling windows). Generated by `backtest/qqq_strategy_sweep.py`.*

> **The Buy & Hold and NDX-own[T+2] rows are a validation, not new information: they reproduce [Table 1](core-trend-signal.md)'s 1x tier exactly** (Buy & Hold 11.65%, NDX own signal [T+2] 13.89%) — Table 1's 1x tier already runs on QQQ's 0.20% expense ratio, so this table and that one are the same computation seen twice. The genuinely new data here is the dual-signal and trailing-stop rows, which Table 1 never tested at 1x. **Dual-signal agreement (no T+2) is the best setup in the table** at 14.69% Avg TWR with only 9 trades — beating every single-signal variant and Buy & Hold, consistent with [Table 4](dual-signal-agreement.md)'s 3x finding that dual-signal agreement is the strongest signal-generation rule. The trailing stop, as elsewhere, trades return for a shallower floor: Worst DD improves from -35.77% (no stop) to -24.94% (with stop) at the cost of ~2pp Avg TWR and roughly double the trades (9-11 -> 18).

| Dual-signal agreement [T+2] | 14.23% | 14.49% | 8.21% | -37.84% | 9 |

> **Caveats:** this uses `^NDX` index data scaled to QQQ's 0.20% expense ratio, not QQQ's own post-1999 price history (QQQ itself didn't exist before 1999, so the full 1986-2000 rolling-window range is only reachable via the index proxy — consistent with how the rest of this project's 1x tier is computed). Overlapping-window caveat applies.
