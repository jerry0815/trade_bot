# Options Overlay (Collar on the Trend Model)

[← Back to README](../../README.md) · Related: [Methodology](methodology.md) · [Core Trend Signal](core-trend-signal.md) · [Tax Treatment](tax-treatment.md)

A research overlay that layers an options structure on top of the [core trend strategy](core-trend-signal.md), asking: *which* option overlay best complements a leveraged (3× Nasdaq / TQQQ) trend-follower? It is a self-contained [`options/`](../../options/) package with its own event-driven backtester, **independent of the production `bot.py` recommendation**. After fixing the option-pricing model and testing five structures across the vol assumption and every major crash since 2001, the answer is a **collar** — sell a ~20Δ call and buy a ~15Δ put on the TQQQ held, monthly, in the bull/transition regimes. Full engine spec, guardrails, and accounting: [`options/README.md`](../../options/README.md).

---

## Final Decision

**Sell the top, protect the bottom — never sell the bottom.** The collar beats plain Trend and every other overlay on a risk-adjusted basis, and the edge is *structural* (it survives the pricing-vol assumption) rather than fitted. But the entire result rests on trend-following: the SMA200 cash-rotation is what makes leveraged Nasdaq survivable; the overlay is a second-order refinement that trades a little of the best months for a materially smoother ride. Belongs in a **tax-advantaged account** (everything is short-term).

| Overlay | Calmar | Verdict |
| :--- | ---: | :--- |
| **Collar** (sell ~20Δ call + buy ~15Δ put) | **4.2–4.4** | **Adopted (research)** — best risk-adjusted, vol-robust |
| Covered calls (sell ~20Δ call) | 3.39 | Runner-up — smoother ride, ~same return |
| Trend (no options) | 2.82 | The load-bearing baseline |
| Two-sided dynamic engine | 2.23 | Rejected — short puts add tail risk |
| Buy & Hold 3× | 0.42 | Uninvestable through a real bear |

## Backtest Results

Real TQQQ prices + `^VXN`; options priced at TQQQ's realistic vol (`^VXN × 2.5`, measured median 2.51×); realized option P&L compounds into the book. See [`options/README.md`](../../options/README.md) for the full accounting.

### Table 10: Options overlay on the trend model (2018-01 → 2026-08)

*$10,000 lump sum. Models 2–4 share the identical 3-state equity sleeve, so any difference is the overlay alone. Generated by `python -m options.run_benchmark`.*

| Model | CAGR | Max DD | Sharpe | Calmar |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Buy & Hold TQQQ | 34.2% | -81.8% | 0.71 | 0.42 |
| Trend (no options) | 93.8% | -33.3% | 1.59 | 2.82 |
| Covered Calls | 92.9% | -27.4% | 1.75 | 3.39 |
| Two-Sided Dynamic | 97.4% | -43.6% | 1.54 | 2.23 |
| **Collar (P.15)** | 93.5% | -22.4% | 2.24 | **4.18** |
| **Collar (P.20)** | 89.1% | -20.2% | 2.32 | **4.41** |

> **The collar wins by drawdown reduction, not option profit.** Covered calls cut drawdown vs plain Trend by capping the top and cushioning with premium (net option P&L is actually slightly *negative*); the collar adds a self-financed protective put that cuts drawdown further. The two-sided engine is *worst* of the overlays because its short-**put** structures add left-tail risk (-44% DD). The collar's Calmar edge holds across the pricing-vol range (2.0–3.0× VXN: Calmar 4.1–4.8) because it is self-financing — the call and put are priced off the same vol, so their vol sensitivities cancel, and the drawdown protection is mechanical (strike-based).

### Table 11: Reconstructed-TQQQ stress test (2001-06 → 2026-08)

*Real TQQQ starts 2010, so this uses a synthetic series rebuilt from `^NDX` (3× minus expense/financing drag, validated at ~0.999 daily-return correlation vs real TQQQ). Captures the dot-com collapse and 2008. Generated by `options/reconstruct_tqqq.py`.*

| Model | CAGR | Max DD | Calmar | DD dot-com | DD 2008 |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Buy & Hold TQQQ | 13.6% | **-98.6%** | 0.14 | -96.9% | -95.0% |
| Trend (no options) | 75.9% | -33.4% | 2.27 | -20.7% | -24.7% |
| Covered Calls | 77.3% | -27.4% | 2.82 | -16.5% | -21.7% |
| Collar (P.15) | 75.7% | -22.4% | 3.38 | -14.9% | -13.9% |
| Collar (P.20) | 71.8% | -20.2% | 3.56 | -14.2% | **-12.4%** |

> **Trend-following is what makes leveraged Nasdaq survivable, and the collar earns its keep in real bears.** A 3× Nasdaq ETF held through the dot-com crash draws down -98.6% — a near-total wipeout; the SMA200 cash-rotation cuts that to -33%. The collar's protective put, which the 2018–2026 window could barely test, cut the 2008 drawdown to -12% vs Trend's -25%. Note every *trend-based* model's overall Max DD is the **2020 COVID crash** (fast, un-trackable by a 200-day average), not dot-com/2008 (slow, rotated to cash early) — which is why the overall Max DD matches the 2018–2026 table; the new pre-2010 data is exercised by the crash-window columns.

## Caveats

- **Strategy-comparison signal, not tradeable P&L, and not investment advice.** No real option chain was used — options are cash-settled at intrinsic, and pricing assumes `^VXN × 2.5` with a fixed 1.2× put skew.
- **Frictions unmodeled:** bid/ask, commissions on 285+ trades/yr, slippage, early assignment, liquidity. **Ignore the absolute ending dollars** — frictionless multi-year compounding is fantasy; only the drawdowns, Sharpe/Calmar, and ranking are meaningful.
- **Pre-2010 data is reconstructed** (validated at ~0.999 correlation, but a model); the collar's edge is largest when crashes actually occur (2001–2026 had four).
- **Taxable accounts erase much of the edge** — short-term rates take ~65% of terminal wealth at 35%; use a tax-advantaged account. See [Tax Treatment](tax-treatment.md).
